**Alps SKCM Blue**, **complicated blue Alps**, or simply **blue Alps**, is a clicky switch in the [Alps SKCL/SKCM series](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Alps_SKCL/SKCM_series "Alps SKCL/SKCM series").

## Description

Complicated blue Alps are highly regarded for their smooth feel. The reason for this is the now-discontinued Threebond TB2581P lubricant.[[1]](https://deskthority.net#cite_note-Threebond-lube-1) Before the name was known, Sandy reported finding the dry lubricant applied to the sliders of an [Acer KB-101A](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Acer_KB-101A "Acer KB-101A").[[2]](https://deskthority.net#cite_note-Sandy-lube-2)

Blue Alps switches are normally branded only on the bottom. Identification is assisted by the unusual greyish-blue colour of the [slider](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Slider "Slider"); while there are [Alps clone](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Alps_clone "Alps clone") switches with blue sliders, none of them have the same shade of blue slider, preferring teal and vivid blue.

The [switchplate](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Switchplate "Switchplate") is typically grey full-height, Rarely black full-height, also white (Short and full-height) switchplates have been found in blue Alps switches.

## Specifications

The following image, taken by alps.tw,[[3]](https://deskthority.net#cite_note-KBT-visit-3) contains the only known official documentation on the blue Alps switch:

[![Catalogue or brochure specifications for Alps SKCM Blue (SKCMAG) and SKCMAF (unknown at the time of writing); from the shadows, the photo appears to show…](https://deskthority.net/wiki/images/a/aa/Alps_SKCMAF_and_SKCMAG_specifications.jpg)](https://deskthority.net/wiki/File:Alps_SKCMAF_and_SKCMAG_specifications.jpg)

The image shows a heading of "Mechanical Contact with Tactile CM(KCM) Series"; it is likely that Alps meant to write "Mechanical Contact with Tactile *Feedback*: CM(KCM) Series" or "Mechanical Contact with *Tactility*: CM(KCM) Series". "CM Series" here refers to the switches. KCM Series may be a general term for all Alps-made keyboards with these switches.

**"Pale/Sky" Blue Variant**

At least three examples have been found of unique Blue Alps in Acer keyboards containing sliders with a paler "sky" blue color and ridges on the inside of the stem. They have tentatively been named "Pale" or "Sky" Blue Alps.

The first example was found in a Tektronix rebranded Acer KB-101A/TK [[4]](https://deskthority.net#cite_note-SkyBlueAlps1-4), a second example was found in an Acer branded 6023 [[5]](https://deskthority.net#cite_note-SkyBlueAlps2-5) and a third example was found in an Acer branded KB-101A [[6]](https://deskthority.net#cite_note-SkyBlueAlps3-6).

## Clones

[Simplified Alps Type III](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Simplified_Alps_Type_III "Simplified Alps Type III") is a clone of the Alps SKCMAG. The switch appears to be an earlier [Hua-Jie AK-CN2](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Hua-Jie_AK_series "Hua-Jie AK series") adapted to use a shell derived from an Alps SKCMAG; unlike most clones, Type III is clearly intended to fool people into mistaking it for a real Alps product, aided by Alps having neglected to visibly brand their switches.

The most obvious difference between the clone and genuine article is that the clone switch has a vivid blue slider, instead of the blue-grey of the Alps product. The clone switch replicates the telltale slits in the slider hole as well as the long side tabs that normally indicate a genuine Alps switch. The upper shell may be derived directly from an Alps moulding, as a plastic support plate was required to support the contact plates where normally the shell has dedicated recesses. A similar switch has been found with a custom shell with the proper recesses.
